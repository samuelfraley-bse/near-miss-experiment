### SLIDE 7 ###
--------------------------------------------------
Random assignment to one of the four conditions

+-50%

This is a between-subjects design (each participant experiences only one condition) because:
-Avoids demand effects: If participants see both skill and luck framing, they might guess the hypothesis
- Avoids carryover: The experience of one framing would contaminate the other
- Cleaner causal inference: Each observation is independent
- Practical: Shorter survey per participant

### SLIDE 8 ###
--------------------------------------------------
Analysis Plan & Data Sample

Analysis
2×2 between-subjects ANOVA testing the Framing × Feedback interaction on willingness to persist

If interaction is significant: simple effects tests within each framing condition to examine near-miss vs. clear-loss contrast

Manipulation checks: independent t-tests verifying skill and feedback perception

All tests two-tailed, α = .05; report 95% CIs and effect sizes

Data Sample
Ideally, 200 total, with 50 in each of the 4 conditions (80% power to detect interaction effect, given our power analysis)

Realistically, expect less than that (128 total, or 38 per cell)

Recruit inside the class, respective master's cohort, general BSE student body as well as friends/family outside BSE

### SLIDE 12 ###
--------------------------------------------------
Near-miss feedback will increase willingness to persist more strongly when the task is framed as skill-based than when framed as luck-based. Specifically, we predict an interaction: the difference between near-miss and clear-loss conditions will be larger when outcomes are attributed to skill versus chance.

### SLIDE 14 ###
--------------------------------------------------
Theory behind expected results

Theory: Conditional reinforcement + skill attribution
Near-misses signal achievable success
But only feel "achievable" when you think YOU can control the outcome

Predictions:
Skill/Near-miss >> Skill/Clear-loss (LARGE gap)
Luck/Near-miss > Luck/Clear-loss (SMALL gap)
Interaction significant - skill amplifies the near-miss effect

Analysis
2×2 ANOVA testing interaction
Simple effects tests
95% CIs for all comparisons

Insight
Whether skill attribution is necessary for strong near-miss effects
